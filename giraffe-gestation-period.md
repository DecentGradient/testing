# Giraffe Gestation and Birth Facts

## Gestation Period
A female giraffe's pregnancy lasts between **14 to 15 months** (around 453 to 464 days). They typically give birth to single calves, and in the wild, births are often synchronized during the dry season to increase survival rates.

## The Dramatic Birth
Mothers give birth standing up, resulting in the newborn calf dropping **5 to 6 feet** (nearly 2 meters) to the ground. This drop is crucial for the calf's survival as the impact breaks the amniotic sac, snaps the umbilical cord, and helps stimulate the calf's first breath.

## Newborn Stats & Physical Traits
At birth, a calf is already massive:
- **Height:** 5.5 to 6.6 feet tall (1.7 to 2 meters)
- **Weight:** 100 to 154 lbs (47 to 70 kg)
- **"Hoof Slippers":** Calves are born with a soft, rubbery covering over their hooves to protect the mother during birth. These fall off shortly after birth.
- **Ossicones (Horns):** The ossicones lay flat against the skull at birth to prevent injury to the mother, standing upright within a few days.

## Rapid Mobility and Growth
Giraffe calves develop incredibly quickly:
- **Walking:** They can stand unassisted within **30 to 60 minutes**.
- **Running:** Within a few hours, the calf is strong enough to run with its mother.
- **Growth Rate:** In the first week of life, calves grow about **1.2 inches (3 centimeters) per day**.

## Early Life & "Nurseries"
For the first 1 to 3 weeks, a calf will spend most of its time hiding in tall grass while the mother stands guard. As they grow older, they join a "crèche" (a giraffe daycare) where one adult female watches over several calves while the other mothers forage.
